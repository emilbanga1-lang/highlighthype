Our Story

It started in the driveway

It happened in the driveway. Just Nicholas, a basketball, and a hoop he'd been attacking all afternoon.

Then — finally — he did it. His first dunk.

And the second his feet hit the pavement, he had the thought every kid who's ever made a great play has had:

There was nobody here to see it.

Not "that was cool." Not even "I did it." The play of his life — and its only witness was the driveway. But Nicholas took it one step further, the step that became all of this:

What if everyone could have seen that… and voted on how cool it was?

Post the play. Everybody watches. Everybody votes. That wasn't a business plan — it was a 9-year-old in a driveway, wishing his moment had an audience.

So his family built it. His dad stayed up late, writing it line by line. His mom became the creative eye — if it shines gold, she approved it. His sister Juliette made sure the girls got a board of their own. His cousin Nicole dreamed up turning a kid's best frame into illustrated art.

And not just the wins — all of it. The loss that stings for a week. The strikeout that teaches you more than the home run ever could. The comeback nobody saw coming. The underdog who refused to quit. Sports are emotional — that's exactly why they matter. Around here, the tears and the lessons count right alongside the trophies.

And then the driveway got bigger. A martial artist's montage. A softball catcher tagging out a runner sliding in at home. Plays from kids they'd never met — each one somebody's dunk in the driveway, except this time, people saw it. And voted on how cool it was. Exactly like he pictured.

Because every kid has that play. The diving catch, caught on one parent's phone and seen by almost no one. The buzzer-beater, buried in a camera roll by Monday morning. Those moments don't need a stadium. They just need somebody to see them — and say that was cool.

Every athlete deserves the hype.

HighlightHype is where young athletes post their best plays for the world to watch, cheer, and vote on — with one athlete crowned every month. And because a play is never just a play, you can mark what it was: a first ever, a personal best, a comeback — even a great effort in a game you lost. Around here, the moment matters as much as the highlight.

Every play is reviewed by a real person before it goes live, and a parent or guardian is always in control. We built it that way because these are our kids too.
